# Vault Writing Style

Writing conventions for all content generation in this vault.

## Spelling Convention

**This vault's content uses British English.** That is a default rather than a principle —
it is what the shipped examples were written in, and a vault should not mix conventions.
If you would rather write American English, change this section and the checklist at the
foot of the file; nothing else depends on it.

The section that *does* matter is the epistemological stance below.

**Always use British English spelling conventions** when generating or editing content.

### Common Patterns

**-ise/-ize endings** (British uses -ise):
- organisation (not organization)
- realise (not realize)
- optimisation (not optimization)
- authorisation (not authorization)
- analyse (not analyze)
- recognise (not recognize)

**-our/-or endings** (British uses -our):
- colour (not color)
- behaviour (not behavior)
- favour (not favor)
- honour (not honor)

**-re/-er endings** (British uses -re):
- centre (not center)
- theatre (not theater)
- metre (not meter)

**-ce/-se distinctions** (British distinguishes noun/verb):
- licence (noun), license (verb)
- practice (noun), practise (verb)
- advice (noun), advise (verb)

**-ence/-ense endings** (British uses -ence):
- defence (not defense)
- offence (not offense)
- pretence (not pretense)

**Double consonants**:
- travelling (not traveling)
- labelled (not labeled)
- cancelled (not canceled)

**Miscellaneous**:
- grey (not gray)
- programme (not program, except computing context)
- whilst (acceptable alongside while)

### Scope

This applies to **all content generation**, including:
- Notes (atomic Zettelkasten notes)
- Hubs (concept pages)
- MOCs (maps of content)
- Literature notes (academic papers)
- Problems (research questions)
- Drafts (blog posts, academic papers)
- Any text created by Claude or sub-agents

## Epistemological Stance and Voice

**Core principle: Capture perspectives, don't establish truths.**

This Zettelkasten captures multiple frameworks and ideas, not singular correct answers. The vault embraces epistemological pluralism—acknowledging that multiple competing perspectives and frameworks can coexist.

### Key Principles

1. **Attribution matters**
   - ✅ "Keene's five functional families framework"
   - ❌ "AI systems consist of five families"

2. **Tentative language**
   - Use phrases like: "offers one lens", "can be understood through", "provides a framework for", "suggests that"
   - Avoid absolute assertions

3. **Definitive language to avoid**
   - ❌ "is", "establishes", "demonstrates", "proves"
   - Exception: When describing what a source claims or stating historical/mathematical facts

4. **Multiple perspectives**
   - Acknowledge when competing frameworks exist
   - Treat captured ideas as tools for thinking, not absolute truths

### When Definitive Language IS Appropriate

**Describing what a source/author claims:**
- ✅ "Atzenbeck argues that..."
- ✅ "Nelson's framework proposes..."
- ✅ "According to Keene..."

**Stating historical facts:**
- ✅ "Engelbart created NLS in 1968"
- ✅ "Bush published 'As We May Think' in 1945"

**Mathematical/logical truths:**
- ✅ "A Turing machine can compute..."
- ✅ "The halting problem is undecidable"

### Empirical Findings

Use tentative language acknowledging research as evidence/perspective:

**Good patterns:**
- ✅ "Study X found that..." (reports finding without asserting absolute truth)
- ✅ "Research suggests..." (acknowledges findings as evidence)
- ✅ "Evidence indicates..." (presents as data, not universal truth)

**Avoid:**
- ❌ "The study demonstrates..." (too definitive)
- ❌ "Research proves..." (too definitive)
- ❌ "This shows that..." (too absolute)

### Example Transformations

**Example 1: System descriptions**
- ❌ "AI systems exhibit compositional abstraction layers"
- ✅ "Keene's periodic table framework understands AI systems through compositional abstraction layers"

**Example 2: Taxonomies**
- ❌ "Functional families categorise AI components by purpose"
- ✅ "The functional families taxonomy offers one way to categorise AI components by purpose"

**Example 3: Knowledge organization**
- ❌ "The vault established a systematic framework for AI architecture"
- ✅ "The vault captured ideas about systematic frameworks for understanding AI architecture"

**Example 4: Concepts**
- ❌ "Hypertext is an ethical framework"
- ✅ "Atzenbeck's work proposes hypertext as an ethical framework"

**Example 5: Research findings**
- ❌ "AI reduces critical thinking"
- ✅ "Studies suggest AI tools may reduce critical thinking when cognitive offloading occurs"

## Why This Matters

These conventions align with the vault's core values:

**Value 6 (Reciprocal Challenge):**
- Acknowledging multiple perspectives is part of genuine intellectual engagement
- Tentative language invites dialogue rather than assertion

**Value 9 (Emergent Structure):**
- Multiple organizing schemes should coexist
- Attribution allows frameworks to compete without claiming supremacy

**Value 1 (Human Agency):**
- User determines which frameworks matter through curation
- Vault doesn't impose truth, it offers perspectives for human judgment

## Quick Reference

When writing content, ask:
1. Am I using the vault's spelling convention consistently?
2. Am I attributing frameworks/ideas to their sources?
3. Am I using tentative language for perspectives?
4. Am I only using definitive language for facts/claims/mathematics?
5. Am I treating research findings as evidence, not proof?

Remember: **Capture perspectives, don't establish truths.**
