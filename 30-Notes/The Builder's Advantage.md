# The Builder's Advantage

> [!tldr] TLDR
> **Key**:: A knowledge system that works best for whoever built it may have succeeded rather than failed to generalise

A system built by its primary user invites an obvious methodological objection: the reported benefits may be contingent on the builder's cognitive style, and the work therefore does not transfer.

Millard reports the pattern that prompts the objection. He describes the system as transformatively useful for his own practice — changing how he engages with literature, thinks about concepts and approaches unfamiliar areas — while observing that colleagues shown the same vault are interested but do not experience the same click. His explanation is that although the core idea transfers, using it produces something bespoke almost immediately: it comes to fit how a particular person thinks, structures the world and talks about it. Many of his own notes originate not in ingested papers but in his own formulations, which then act as "lodestones" the rest of the network is built around. To another reader those notes are simply notes.

The stronger reading he offers draws on Anderson and Millard's argument that what unites hypertext's diverse forms is not a technology but an ideology — a requirement for non-regularity, rooted in post-structuralism and carrying existentialist values. If existence precedes essence, meaning is not given but constructed through choice. On that reading a system that works best for its builder has not failed to generalise; it has demanded the commitment hypertext has always asked of its users.

The design implication is not that everyone must start from nothing, but that a system must leave genuine room to be reshaped — consequential decisions about structure and vocabulary, not a preferences panel.

## Details

- Reframes an apparent methodological weakness as a substantive claim
- Rests on a contested reading of hypertext as ideological rather than technical
- Sits awkwardly beside publishing the architecture, as Millard acknowledges
- Predicts that a user who changes nothing will find the system flat — and that this is informative
