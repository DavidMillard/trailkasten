# Curation as Learning

> [!tldr] TLDR
> **Key**:: In one sustained practice, the provenance of AI-assisted decisions faded while the knowledge itself was retained and reinforced through use

Research on the **AI Memory Gap** suggests that when AI output is context-specific, immediately editable and integrated into a working environment, the source-monitoring cues that normally distinguish self-generated from externally-generated content are weakened. Users become unable to say reliably what they decided and what the system proposed.

Millard reports exactly this, with an important qualification. For a note made six months earlier he cannot say whether it was proposed by the system or requested by him — that distinction is described as genuinely gone. But the *contents* are not. He reports remembering what the vault holds "remarkably well, considering how big it is", and routinely interrupting a session to observe that a proposed note duplicates an existing one or connects to something elsewhere.

The suggested reading is that curation functions as a mode of learning rather than of filing. The act of deciding whether a concept belongs is what installs it; subsequent use reinforces it. What is lost is authorship attribution; what is kept is the knowledge. Millard's summary is that he "expected a filing system and got something closer to a study aid".

This complicates the cognitive-offloading worry in a specific way: offloading appears to have occurred for provenance, but not for content.

## Details

- Provenance and content appear to decay at very different rates
- The retention seems tied to having made the decision, not to having read the material
- One practitioner's report over roughly four months — suggestive, not evidence
- Would be testable: recall of note contents versus recall of who proposed them
