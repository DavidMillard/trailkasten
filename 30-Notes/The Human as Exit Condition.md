# The Human as Exit Condition

> [!tldr] TLDR
> **Key**:: A generative knowledge system has no internal stopping point, so the decision to stop must come from outside it

Suggest-only workflows — where a system proposes and a person selects — are usually justified on grounds of agency: the user should decide, not the machine. Millard offers a second and less obvious reason, which is structural rather than ethical.

A knowledge system that generates without human selection would generate indefinitely. Each new note implies connections to notes that do not yet exist; those could be created, and would imply further connections in turn. The expansion is recursive and there is no natural terminating condition anywhere inside it.

What the human supplies is that terminus: the judgement that this is the boundary of present interest, and that further expansion would produce diminishing returns. Millard's claim is that nothing in the system can determine this, because it is not a property of the material — it is a property of what the person currently cares about.

If this holds, human selection in such systems is not a safeguard bolted on for reassurance. It is load-bearing: remove it and the structure does not become autonomous, it becomes unbounded.

## Details

- Distinct from the agency argument, though usually bundled with it
- The stopping point is a fact about the user's interests, not about the source material
- Implies that "how much should this generate?" has no correct answer in principle
- Also acts as a filter on coherence: unbounded growth would dilute the network
