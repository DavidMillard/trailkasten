# Structural Scaffolds Make AI Output Correctable

> [!tldr] TLDR
> **Key**:: Without a structure that gives each generated unit a declared role, AI output can only be accepted or rejected wholesale

A recurring difficulty with generative writing tools is that their output arrives as undifferentiated prose. The reader's options are to accept it or reject it entire, and Millard suggests most people accept, because reading a passage critically enough to reject part of it costs more effort than writing it oneself would have done.

A three-layer scaffold — atomic notes, hubs, Maps of Content — appears to change this. Every generated unit occupies a declared position in a knowledge network, which makes it individually addressable. One link can be argued with. One note can be rejected while seven are kept. Revision happens at the granularity of a single idea rather than a whole document.

The claim is not that structure constrains what a model can express. It is that structure governs *how* that expression enters the reader's thinking — and that this turns out to matter more than the quality of the prose. Millard identifies this as the point he would most want others to take from the system.

## Details

- The problem is one of reviewability, not of generation quality
- Wholesale accept-or-reject is a failure of granularity, and granularity is a property of structure
- Explicit links make proposed relationships visible and therefore debatable
- Suggests scaffolds matter more as AI output improves, not less — fluent prose is harder to read critically
